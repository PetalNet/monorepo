## Question

Parker has ruled the hard part already: money, sending anything as Parker, and deletions need a
"Was this you?" tap on his phone, every time, on every channel, and nothing spoken counts as
approval. The Nov 6 page adds that other approvals still work by voice on a private channel.

What is left to settle is the edge: which actions fall in the taps-only set beyond those three
(standing orders, recurring charges, anything irreversible), what happens when the tap never comes,
and whether a voice approval on headphones really is enough for everything else.

## Resolution

Already ruled by Parker on 2026-10-28, the day after the rule it overturned, in a DM that never
reached the published page. Not re-asked.

- Approval by voice counts when it is Parker. A phone call is him.
- Headphones carry approval, checked against his known devices, not a name guess. His words: "In
  headphones should be approvaled. just speaker is messy."
- On a room speaker she asks him to confirm on his phone or to put headphones on.
- **There is no carve-out for money, sending anything as Parker, or deletions.** His Oct 28 wording
  admits none, so a voice approval on a private channel covers them like anything else.

### Corrected 2026-11-11, twice

This ticket first recorded "the three always need a tap on his phone, every time, on every channel",
labelled as his ruling. **It was never his.** The label came from the Nov 6 tiers page, which asserts
it while also listing it as its own first open question, and the compiled record contains only the
permissive direction above. It was briefly downgraded to reasoning, then removed outright: the
permissive reading *is* the ruling.

**That propagated.** The rule requiring a phone tap to place an outbound call was derived from this
one, so it went too; see [What a stranger gets on the phone](what-a-stranger-gets.md).

Anything stricter is a **recommendation to pitch with evidence**, not a question to re-ask. For money
the evidence exists: a journalist entered his own bank account by phone using a voice clone trained on
about five minutes of speech (Cox / Lloyds, Feb 2023), and ASVspoof 2021 found replay detection did not
survive real rooms. His ruling turns on "as long as its me", and that is the premise a clone defeats.
**Until he rules otherwise his ruling stands as written.**

The Oct 27 rule that "nothing spoken counts as your approval", and hard rule R4's "nothing that
arrives by voice counts as consent", are **retired**. They were already dead by implication from his
own lamp ruling, and dead explicitly by the Oct 28 DM.
