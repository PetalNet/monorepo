The first viewport pairs an uninterrupted photograph of the physical object with a catalog number, type, variety, ownership state and claim action. On phones the photograph sits above the label. Claim opens a short inline name form; release uses a confirmation dialog because it changes who can claim the physical item.

Charcoal green (#171b19) recedes behind the photographs. Warm paper (#f2f0e7) is the alternate theme and photograph caption surface. Herb green (#c2d49b dark, #3c552a light) marks collection actions and specimen numbers. Geist, self-hosted through fontless, keeps the label legible and gives numbers a consistent tabular rhythm. No letter spacing overrides.

The number is deliberately oversized because it identifies an individual physical item. Hairline rules organize catalog fields; filled cards hold objects in a collection. Small 2px corners recall specimen labels. 8px spacing units keep the labels, forms and admin tools aligned. Generous space around artwork keeps the physical object as the focal point. Lucide mushroom icons stand for uncollected varieties; these are explicitly silhouettes, never substitute artwork. Controls have short state transitions and reduced-motion support.

The design keeps real photos, real specimen data and private ownership. No public owner lists, social rankings, invented lore or count claims. The concept seed was run, and the user-pinned museum direction takes precedence over its unrelated challengers.
